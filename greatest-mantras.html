Ten Timeless Mantras

Each mantra below has been chanted by millions across centuries. Choose one that resonates — the best mantra is the one you return to daily.

Vedic · Universal
ॐ — Om (Aum)

Pronounced: A-U-M — three sounds flowing into silence

The primordial sound. In Vedic tradition, Om is the vibration from which the universe arises — encompassing creation (A), preservation (U), dissolution (M), and the silence beyond.

How to practiceChant slowly on a long exhale, letting "A" rise from the belly, "U" fill the chest, and "M" hum in the head. Rest in the silence after each repetition.
Tibetan Buddhist
ॐ मणि पद्मे हूँ — Om Mani Padme Hum

Pronounced: ohm mah-nee pahd-may hoom

"The jewel is in the lotus." The mantra of Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of compassion. Each of its six syllables is said to purify one realm of suffering and cultivate boundless compassion.

How to practiceRepeat softly or silently while visualizing warmth radiating from your heart to all beings. Traditionally counted in rounds of 108.
Vedic · Rig Veda
Gayatri Mantra

Om Bhur Bhuvah Svaha, Tat Savitur Varenyam, Bhargo Devasya Dhimahi, Dhiyo Yo Nah Prachodayat

Pronounced: ohm bhoor bhoo-vah svah-hah, taht sah-vee-toor vah-rayn-yum, bhar-go day-vahs-yah dhee-mah-hee, dhee-yo yo nah prah-cho-dah-yaht

"We meditate on the radiant light of the Divine; may it illuminate our minds." Over 3,000 years old, the Gayatri is considered the most sacred verse of the Vedas — a prayer for awakened intelligence.

How to practiceTraditionally chanted at sunrise. Learn the melody first by listening, then chant 3, 9, or 108 times facing the morning light.
Vedic · Breath Mantra
सो ऽहम् — So Hum

Pronounced: soh hum

"I am That." A natural mantra said to ride on the breath itself — So on the inhale, Hum on the exhale. It affirms the unity of the individual self with the universal.

How to practiceThe easiest mantra for beginners. Silently think "So" as you breathe in and "Hum" as you breathe out. No counting needed — just follow the breath.
Shaivite Hindu
ॐ नमः शिवाय — Om Namah Shivaya

Pronounced: ohm nah-mah shee-vah-yah

"I bow to Shiva" — the inner Self, the consciousness that dwells in all. Known as the five-syllable mantra (panchakshara), it is one of the most beloved mantras in all of Hinduism.

How to practiceChant aloud in a steady rhythm, then gradually let it become a silent inner repetition. Excellent for dissolving stress and ego-tension.
Vaishnava · Bhakti
Maha Mantra (Hare Krishna)

Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna Hare Hare, Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama Hare Hare

Pronounced: hah-ray krish-nah · hah-ray rah-mah

The "great mantra" of devotion. In Bhakti tradition, chanting the divine names is itself the meditation — the sound carries joy, surrender, and love directly into the heart.

How to practiceSing it, chant it, or repeat it silently — devotional mood matters more than technique. Often practiced with kirtan (call-and-response singing).
Nichiren Buddhist
南無妙法蓮華經 — Nam Myoho Renge Kyo

Pronounced: nahm myoh-hoh ren-gay kyoh

"Devotion to the Mystic Law of the Lotus Sutra." The core practice of Nichiren Buddhism — chanted to awaken one's innate Buddha nature and transform suffering into wisdom and courage.

How to practiceChant aloud in a steady, energetic rhythm — traditionally with eyes open, focused and determined, for anywhere from five minutes to an hour.
Kundalini · Sikh Tradition
ਸਤਿ ਨਾਮੁ — Sat Nam

Pronounced: saht nahm

"Truth is my identity." A seed (bija) mantra central to Kundalini yoga — Sat (truth) on the inhale, Nam (name/identity) on the exhale, aligning you with your truest self.

How to practiceMentally vibrate "Sat" as you inhale and "Nam" as you exhale, or chant aloud with "Sat" long and "Nam" short: Saaaaaat Nam.
Heart Sutra · Mahayana Buddhist
Gate Gate Paragate Parasamgate Bodhi Svaha

Pronounced: gah-tay gah-tay pah-rah-gah-tay pah-rah-sahm-gah-tay boh-dee svah-hah

"Gone, gone, gone beyond, gone utterly beyond — awakening, so be it!" The closing mantra of the Heart Sutra, celebrating the journey across the ocean of suffering to the shore of enlightenment.

How to practiceRecite slowly and contemplatively, feeling each "gate" as a letting-go — of worry, of grasping, of the small self.
Universal Peace Mantra
ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः — Om Shanti Shanti Shanti

Pronounced: ohm shahn-tee shahn-tee shahn-tee

"Om, peace, peace, peace." The threefold repetition invokes peace in body, mind, and spirit — or peace from the world, from others, and from within oneself.

How to practicePerfect for closing any meditation. Chant once, letting each "shanti" be softer than the last, until the third dissolves into silence.

How to Practice Mantra Meditation

1

Choose One Mantra

Pick a single mantra and stay with it for at least 40 days. Depth comes from repetition, not variety.

2

Sit Comfortably

Sit with a tall, relaxed spine — on a cushion, chair, or floor. Close your eyes and take three slow breaths.

3

Repeat & Return

Chant aloud, whisper, or repeat silently. When the mind wanders (it will), gently return to the sound.

4

Count 108

Tradition counts repetitions on a 108-bead mala. One full round is one "mala." Use the counter below.
